The objective of this project is to identify the most economically stable and investment-ready countries for international real estate expansion. By analyzing a comprehensive set of economic indicators across 34 OECD countries, the project aims to determine which variables most significantly influence public debt—a key measure of fiscal health, risk, and long-term stability.


BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING
Introduction 
Horizon Development has achieved significant success in commercial real estate development, management, and brokerage operations across the United States. Building on this expertise, the company now plans to expand its operations internationally. This analysis provides a detailed assessment of potential candidate countries and identifies those that offer the most promising conditions for successful investment.

Candidates 
The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) is an intergovernmental organization founded in 1961, currently comprising 34 member countries. Its mission is to promote economic growth, stability, and global trade. Membership in the OECD is a strong initial indicator of a country's economic health, commitment to international cooperation, and the availability of reliable data. For this analysis, we begin with the OECD member list as the foundation and conduct further evaluation using key economic indicators to determine the most suitable countries for investment.
The countries included are: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Korea (Republic of), Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Mexico, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Slovak Republic, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey, United Kingdom, and United States.

Economic Indicators
This project analyzes twenty economic variables, with Public Sector Debt as the primary variable of interest. A nation’s debt burden can significantly influence its economic stability and attractiveness for business investment. Therefore, our analysis focuses on how various economic factors impact public debt across countries to guide the selection of the most suitable candidates for international expansion.
At first glance, high public debt may suggest greater financial risk, as debt represents a liability. However, the broader economic context matters. For instance, a country with high debt but also a high GDP may be effectively leveraging debt to stimulate growth and investment, making this a potentially positive indicator. Conversely, a country with low debt and low GDP may face more structural economic challenges. To account for this nuance, we introduce a new variable: the Debt-to-GDP Ratio, which better reflects a country's fiscal health and its capacity to manage debt sustainably.
